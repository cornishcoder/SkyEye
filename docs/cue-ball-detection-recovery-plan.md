# Cue ball detection recovery

8 October 2026 · **First two stages implemented, checked on Pixel and installed in SkyEye Lab; room-lighting acceptance open.** This work continues O08 current-frame recovery alongside the O09 learned-detector comparison. It preserves the C# vision pipeline and canonical GameState.

## Evidence and suspected failure

The user's stationary-table dashboard samples 50–73 contain five frames with fewer than the expected 16 balls. The user confirms that the cue ball remains visible throughout. These counts establish intermittent incomplete layouts, but do not identify which detection stage failed. They are one reported sequence, not a held-out accuracy estimate.

The ordinary detector can recognise one cue ball, derive colour gains from it, and then replace all proposals with a second detection on the colour-corrected image. A valid cue ball can be lost during that replacement or subsequent classification. The absence of the no-reference warning in the reported failing samples points to this path, but paired stage telemetry must confirm it. Local illumination gain extrema alone cannot establish cue ball brightness or contrast.

## Implementation order

1. **Preserve current geometry through colour correction.** Validate cue classifications against raw full-body boundaries at calibrated ball size before they can supply a colour reference. Unsupported bright cloth remains unknown, and a supported black body can be recognised from its actual raw paint. Correct colours on these same current-frame proposals; do not replace the complete geometry list with another Hough pass. This avoids losing reds/yellows while recovering the cue ball. Recheck apparent cue identities after colour correction against current raw body evidence. Record initial, pre-correction, post-correction and final cue counts, rejected cue identities, preserved geometry and boundary support.
2. **Independent neutral-body search.** When the initial pass has no supported cue ball, propose compact neutral/cream bodies from the original pixels, with brightness above their local background, calibrated diameter, roundness/fill and raw-image boundary support. Local illumination smoothing can flatten a dim ball's contrast, so its output cannot be the only proposal image. Check body and rim appearance to exclude the black ball's number patch. Bound new boundary fitting to four plausible bodies; reuse only boundary/classification work from this exact frame. Add empty-cloth, stripes, dark ball with cream patch and exposure regression cases.
3. **Fresh recovery within temporal verification.** Preserve O08's existing separate-frame burst consensus. A last-seen position may later seed a local search through the existing tracking seam, but cannot supply an observed ball. Require support in the newest frame; motion, source/calibration changes and obstruction invalidate reuse. Keep per-frame diagnostics available. Any additional stable dashboard view must identify observed, tracked and uncertain evidence distinctly. This stage follows the measured result of stages 1–2; do not interlace images or assume misses are independent.
4. **Continue O09.** Collect completely labelled, session-separated ordinary/dim/bright/blind-sunlight frames and compare current, learned and hybrid candidates on identical pixels. Freeze configuration/provenance for each run. The first two partial saved label frames remain ineligible. [O09 plan](o09-learned-ball-detection-spike.md).

## Delivered candidate and evidence

The `cue-ball-current-body-v2` candidate uses `FreshCueBallRecovery=true`; `false` retains the original geometry/classification branch for controlled replay. Cue identities require raw full-body support before colour reference selection and after colour correction. A bounded raw neutral/cream body search recovers initial misses. The ordinary colour pass classifies the existing geometry rather than rerunning Hough. Frame-local classification/boundary caches avoid repeated work and contain no history. Neutral cloth's value ceiling follows measured cloth brightness up to 120 instead of the legacy fixed upper limit 90; dim cloth retains its lower derived value. No cue acceptance support threshold was lowered.

The isolated native Pixel checks pass 17 before/after image comparisons plus a movement/removal sequence: three forced initial misses, one forced second-pass miss, four negative scenes, two competing neutral bodies, six existing image fixtures and two partial-label source images. All six existing fixtures have a cue ball in the candidate output; three lacked one in the baseline. Position-matched red/yellow/black detections did not decrease on those fixtures. Both recent source images recover the cue ball, with the cue-labelled frame's recovered centre inside a separately visually reviewed source region. Several images still have unresolved candidates or missing other colours; this is not a whole-table reliability result. See [bounded evidence](evidence/cue-ball-recovery-pixel-checks.json).

Earlier development runs exposed flattened dim contrast, second-pass loss of a red ball, and invalid negative/count-only test assumptions. The final checks use original pixels for recovery, preserve geometry, use soft glare as the unsupported-boundary negative, and compare actual labelled colour/position correspondence. Individual final replay analyses ranged up to approximately 1.33 seconds; variable sequential replay timings do not establish live latency or resource acceptance.
